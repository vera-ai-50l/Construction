Where the original framing needs correcting

→"2x revenue." Revenue adds; it doesn't multiply. The same 325 founder-hours now serve two offers. The gain is timing: lead-gen cash arrives while Vera deals are still in pilots.
→"Cost synergy." Real but small: the whole shared stack is about ₹13.5k a month. The larger synergy is learning, since every copy and signal test informs both engines.
→"Belgium, Switzerland." They're quieter partly because cold email to named people there needs consent. See Countries.
→A hedge needs uncorrelated risk. One shared domain or LinkedIn ban would hit both businesses at once. Separate sending infrastructure is what makes it a hedge.

Vera's three warming inboxes: one check before they send

If those inboxes sit on Vera's main domain (the one on your website and proposals), keep them for warm intros, replies and low-volume cold, about 15 a day each. Put volume on two secondary domains, so a bad week of cold email can never land Lodha or PRS Legal follow-ups in spam.

Planned monthly volume, at 22 sending days: Vera main 3 × 15/day = 990, Vera secondary 4 × 25/day = 2,200, lead gen 8 × 25/day = 4,400. Total 7,590.

Why per-inbox volume stays at 25–30 a day

Instantly's 2026 benchmark recommends ramping new inboxes from 5–10 a day to 15, then 30, and not exceeding 30 campaign emails per inbox per day. Domains need 4–6 weeks of warmup before full volume.

Google and Yahoo (Feb 2024) and Microsoft (May 2025) require SPF, DKIM and DMARC for bulk senders and treat a 0.3% spam-complaint rate as the failure line; Google recommends staying under 0.1%. At 30 sends a day, one complaint is already 3%. That's why scale comes from more inboxes, not more email per inbox.

Why margins are high, and why that won't last for generalists

A practitioner breakdown quoted by Prospeo puts the delivery cost of an $8k retainer at roughly $500 for domains and mailboxes plus $2–3k of execution. The rest is margin.

That margin is under pressure: entry AI SDR tools cost $100–400 a month, so a buyer can now run mediocre outbound alone. What they can't easily copy is a niche-specific signal feed and a pay-for-results guarantee. That's the position to take.

05 · Signal and intentBoth engines

Signals roughly double replies; treat bigger vendor claims as a ceiling.

A signal is a public event that says a company has the problem, and often the budget, right now. Emailing on that event changes who you contact and when, which is why it lifts close rates as well as replies.

Why signals raise close rates, not only replies

①Timing. A job post or a RERA extension → budget or pain exists today → more replies say "yes" instead of "not now".
②Relevance. First line names their own event → fewer "not interested" → higher share of positive replies.
③Deliverability. Fewer, fresher contacts → fewer bounces and complaints → better inbox placement → more replies from the same volume.
④Position. You arrive before the vendor shortlist exists → shorter cycle → more deals close before a competitor appears.

Instantly's data says 58% of replies come from step one, so the signal has to be in the first email, not saved for a follow-up.

06 · FreebiesBoth engines

Give a small, specific result before asking for a meeting.

The freebies that work cost you minutes and prove the engine works. Put the cheapest proof inside the first email, offer the next one in email two, and deliver the expensive ones unasked only to your top accounts.

FreebieTouchYour costWhy it lifts repliesWatch out

Rules for every first email

→Under 80 words: one line on their situation, one value line, one ask.
→No attachments, at most one link, no calendar link in touch one.
→Give before asking: the proof sits in the email, not behind a call.
→Plain text, sender name a real founder, clear opt-out line.
What the evidence says about video and audits

Salesforge's practitioner data puts video and "Loom" asks at 3–5% reply, and specific teardowns at 5–7%. Single-campaign stories of 15–22% exist but are anecdotes.

A video only works when it's specific to the prospect. Record them for your top 20% of accounts, under 90 seconds, and embed a thumbnail rather than a bare link.

07 · Multichannel outboundBoth engines

Run two channels well rather than five badly: email plus LinkedIn over 14 days.

Email alone averages 3–5% reply in 2026; a coordinated email and LinkedIn sequence reports 7–12%. The coordination matters: never email and message on the same day, and a reply on any channel stops every other touch.

India-specific: calls and WhatsApp

Since TRAI's February 2025 amendment, promotional calls belong on registered 140-series numbers, and five complaints in ten days can bar every number a sender holds for 15 days. A founder's own phone is at risk.

So in the Vera sequence, call and WhatsApp only people who replied, accepted on LinkedIn, or came through an introduction.

11 · Setup and the 90-day journeyBoth engines

Test the offer on the warmed inboxes next week; decide on 9 January.

The two pre-warmed mailboxes let lead gen start testing on 17 Nov while new domains warm. Vera's cold sequences start when its inboxes finish warmup around 1 Dec. Three gates decide whether to continue.

Gates

1 DecDeliverability. Bounce under 2%, no blacklist hits, test emails landing in Primary. Else fix before any volume.
25 DecOffer. At least 1% positive replies on 600+ contacts in one segment. Else change the offer or niche once.
9 JanGo or stop. 2 paying clients or 8 held sales calls. Else stop lead gen and point the engine at Vera.
8 FebScale. ₹1.5L+ lead-gen MRR and Vera pipeline not starved. Then buy Sales Navigator and LinkedIn automation.
The step most teams miss: a pre-warmed bench

Each lead-gen client needs their own 3 domains and 9 inboxes, and those need 3–4 weeks of warmup. Without a bench, your first client waits a month for their first meeting.

Buy and warm one client set (about ₹3.2k a month) in early December so the first founding client starts sending the week they sign.
